Michigan has a new senator, and his name is Abdul El-Sayed. The Associated Press called the race at 2:27 in the morning after election day, an hour after he had already walked on stage in Detroit to claim it. He beat Mike Rogers, the former congressman Trump had endorsed early on, for the seat Gary Peters is leaving. By AP's count as of November 25, it ended around 52.5% to 45.5%.

He will be the first Muslim to sit in the US Senate. The son of Egyptian immigrants. Alhamdulillah.

Nobody gave him this

He first had to get through his own party. In the August primary, the Michigan establishment lined up behind Haley Stevens: Governor Whitmer, Senator Peters, the people who usually decide these things. He won by about one point anyway. Back in 2018, Whitmer had beaten him by more than twenty in the race for governor. People who wrote him off then had to watch him come back.

Then the general election, and a campaign that cost well over a hundred million dollars in ads. By mid-October, Republicans had outspent the Democrats on it.

Trump, Vance, and the name

The attacks started the day the primary was called. Trump went with "Radical Left Socialist" and "Communist Loser". Then he got to the part he really wanted to say. He posted a picture of himself with Melania next to one of El-Sayed and his wife, who wears a hijab, captioned "Two VERY DIFFERENT America's". Later in the campaign he called him "Mohammed" and said he "goes back to the days of the Jihad".

JD Vance came to Michigan again and again to do the same job. He called him "evil", a "weasel", and spent about half of one rally speech in Monroe on his background. Rogers, for his part, kept a low profile and let them talk.

El-Sayed did not duck any of it. Back in August he said they would try to paint him a certain way because of his name, and that America is bigger than very small people like Trump, Vance or Rogers want it to be. He even invited Trump to come and campaign in Michigan.

What actually won it

Not the insults. He talked about the cost of living, about health care for everyone, about getting corporate money out of politics, and he brought in young people and voters who usually stay home. Michigan voted for Trump twice, but it has not sent a Republican to the Senate since 1994, and this year it was clearly in no mood to start.

So here we are. They spent months telling Michigan that a Muslim with an Arabic name was a danger to the country, and Michigan answered with a ballot. Habibi made it to the Senate. N'en déplaise à Trump and to his vice president.

Alhamdulillah.
